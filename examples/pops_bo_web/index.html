What the comparison shows

Short answer

In this toy, the structured model provides most of the benefit. POPS + PES adds robustness over the point estimate when the model is clearly misspecified. It does not beat a nonparametric GP that is given the same structural prior.

1 · A structured surrogate extrapolates where a stationary GP must explore

With 8 points in x ∈ [0, 0.5], the parametric model already places its optimum in the last period near x ≈ 1. At P = 0.2, δ = 0.12, POPS + PES has median best-measured regret 0.023 at iteration 3, against 0.139 for GP-RBF + EI. The RBF GP spends roughly 8–10 experiments finding the far peak; at P = 0.45 (spec) there are only ~2 periods, so it needs about 5. The parametric methods win in the scarce-data regime of Lee et al.

2 · POPS represents model-form error, and that error sets a floor

The POPS spread grows with δ and vanishes at δ = 0 (all members collapse onto the true parameters). The same misspecification also biases the location of every member's optimum. No measurement within the model class removes that bias, so all parametric strategies plateau: around 0.02 regret at δ = 0.12 and around 0.09–0.13 at δ = 0.3. The RBF GP passes them after about 10 iterations. The POPS spread in x* is a few grid cells, typically narrower than the bias, so it signals the inadequacy but does not bound it.

3 · PES targets disagreement about x*, not response variance

The acquisition panel overlays POPS predictive sd: PES maxima rarely coincide with the sd maxima. Early on, members often split between the peak near x ≈ 0.09 and the one near x ≈ 1, and PES measures where that split is decided. Against the greedy θ* ablation, this matters when δ is large. At δ = 0.3, P = 0.45, POPS + PES has median 0.091 versus 0.119 and mean 0.097 versus 0.153 at iteration 15, because greedy occasionally re-measures one wrong point forever. At δ ≤ 0.12 the two are tied. The response-variance control (POPS + UCB) does about as well as PES on this problem, so the gain comes from POPS uncertainty more than from the entropy criterion specifically.

Parametric prior versus POPS + PES

  • Gain from the inductive bias: the parametric methods versus GP-RBF in the first 3–8 experiments. The periodic-kernel GP control, with P known, is best on every panel. It has the same structural prior without a fixed functional form, so it has no bias floor. In this toy the prior can be written as a kernel; for a kinetic network as in Lee et al. it generally cannot.
  • Gain from POPS + PES: the difference between POPS + PES and greedy θ*. That difference is zero at δ ≤ 0.12, positive at δ = 0.3, and mostly a reduction in bad-seed tail risk (mean, upper quartile) rather than in the median.
  • Paper-literal PES (grid-scale ε, no exploitation rule) keeps measuring wherever any residual x* disagreement remains. Its recommendation regret matches POPS + PES, but its best-measured regret is much worse. The default variant uses ε = 0.01² (the x* resolution we care about) and measures the predicted optimum once the expected gain falls below 0.05 nats. This generalises the paper's tie-break rule and is our modification.
  • Limitation: with initial data in [0, 0.35] (less than one period), the 8 POPS members can all extrapolate the wrong trend and agree on the wrong peak. In that case H[x*] ≈ 0 and PES has nothing to resolve. POPS only reflects misfit that the observed residuals reveal.
